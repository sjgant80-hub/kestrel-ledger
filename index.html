Why this is not a traditional database

A traditional web app flushes RAM on close and persists aggregate JSON state it hopes it wrote in time. KESTREL stores raw physics: the 6-byte packets and their 64-byte signatures, as ArrayBuffers. The entire state history is kilobytes, not megabytes, and is unreadable to standard browser scraping — a local cryptographic cipher. A browser crash, a power death or an abrupt close after a packet is committed loses nothing: the mathematical coordinate is already in the shadow fold. On boot the femtoLLM reads the immutable DNA, then replays the microscopic ledger — 6-byte packets, so 10,000 past actions fast-forward in milliseconds — and the intelligence resumes mid-thought.

This also gives SENTINEL's bounded replay window a durable home (its v2 flag #1): the window is RAM-only and empty after a reboot, so a recent replay would slip through — but rehydrated from the ledger on inhale, the window is restored and the replay is caught.
